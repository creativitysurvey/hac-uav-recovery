\title{HAC: Node-Level Hybrid Force--Graph Control and Smooth Curriculum Scheduling for Resilient UAV Swarm Connectivity Recovery}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Self-healing communication connectivity (SCC) for unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) swarms requires rapidly reconnecting fractured subnetworks after partial node failures while preserving wide spatial coverage, yet pursuing either objective in isolation directly degrades the other. Recent hybrid frameworks such as R2C address this tension by combining intra-subnetwork virtual-force expansion with subnetwork-level multipartite graph convolution under a steep time-adaptive fusion schedule, which bundles together several unisolated architectural choices. In this work, we formulate a three-axis taxonomy of the SCC design space spanning decision paradigm, spatial reasoning granularity, and trade-off scheduling, and systematically isolate each axis through controlled multi-seed experiments. We propose Hybrid Adaptive Convolution (\textsc{HAC}), a lightweight node-level hybrid controller that fuses a parameter-free buffered virtual-force heuristic with a single global graph convolutional policy at the velocity level without subnetwork partitioning, convex-hull boundary extraction, or multipartite graph construction. Across random and uniform deployment scenarios under up to $80\%$ node failure, \textsc{HAC} under a constant $50/50$ fusion weight outperforms purely heuristic, purely learned, and subnetwork-level rigid-translation baselines with substantially lower cross-seed variance, while retaining $88.7\%$ to $90.7\%$ of the coverage reported by R2C ($6.2$ to $7.7\text{ pp}$ absolute difference) under $80\%$ node loss. Furthermore, we demonstrate that transplanting steep adaptive fusion schedules into simplified hybrid controllers degrades recovery robustness, whereas widening the transition window restores near-optimal recovery performance. Both theoretical analysis and granularity-substitution counter-experiments reveal that the fusion schedule acts as an implicit training curriculum governed by autonomy exposure, which stabilizes node-level policies in a residual-correction regime while failing to rescue subnetwork-level architectures whose rigid output spaces lack node-level expressiveness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Problem Context and Fundamental Trade-Off}
\textbf{Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) swarms deployed in contested or disaster-struck environments face recurring partial node failures that fracture communication topologies and degrade spatial coverage.} Wireless aerial networks provide rapid communication coverage in post-disaster relief, dynamic battlefields, and congested areas \citep{coletta2023auav,shi2025symmetry,wang2022task,wang2024angle}. During operation, internal hardware faults or external attacks can disable a subset of UAVs at any time \citep{wang2024survey,kurunathan2023machine}. When substantial node loss occurs, the surviving communication graph splits into multiple disconnected components, severely disrupting both multi-hop data routing and mission-area coverage \citep{li2023reinforcement,peng2022smarttro}. The self-healing communication connectivity (SCC) problem seeks a velocity control policy that repositions surviving UAVs so that the communication graph regains full connectivity as rapidly as possible while preserving maximal spatial coverage across the mission area.

\textbf{Accelerating topological reconnection and preserving spatial coverage impose directly opposing kinematic demands on surviving agents.} Pulling surviving UAVs inward toward a common center shortens reconnection time but contracts the covered footprint, whereas dispersing UAVs outward preserves area coverage at the risk of slowing or preventing link restoration. Managing this tension becomes increasingly difficult as the failure ratio approaches extreme regimes where surviving nodes are sparsely scattered across the arena.

\subsection{Limitations of Existing Paradigms}
\textbf{Purely heuristic recovery methods require no training data but scale poorly under severe, unstructured node failures.} Classical non-learning approaches either identify critical cut-vertices and trigger cascaded neighbor substitutions \citep{akkaya2009distributed,mi2015obstacle,zhang2024uavenabled} or guide surviving agents toward predefined rendezvous points using artificial potential fields \citep{joshi2012autonomous,shriwastav2018roundtable,chen2020sidr,wang2024apfcpp}. Because these methods rely on static topological assumptions and local interaction rules, they become brittle when large-scale failures isolate components beyond local sensing ranges \citep{tosun2024dpkcr}.

\textbf{Purely learned graph neural network (GNN) policies achieve rapid reconnection but suffer from spatial collapse.} Learning-based methods formulate SCC as a differentiable graph optimization or reinforcement learning problem \citep{mou2022resilient,peng2025prioritized,wang2025uav}. While GNN policies adapt effectively to diverse failure topologies, neighborhood message aggregation biases surviving UAVs to congregate around high-degree hubs, causing severe spatial clustering and coverage loss. Diffusion-based and differential-subgraph convolutions \citep{lin2024multihop,lin2025multihop} partially alleviate clustering by promoting multi-hop dispersion, yet they struggle to maintain stable layouts under extreme fragmentation and rarely enforce inter-agent spacing guarantees.

\subsection{Disentangling Hybrid Recovery Architecture and Scheduling}
\textbf{Recent divide-and-conquer hybrid frameworks achieve strong performance but bundle spatial granularity with steep adaptive scheduling.} To reconcile connectivity and coverage, \citet{peng2026r2c} introduced R2C, which combines a buffered virtual-force module for intra-subnetwork expansion (ISE) with a multipartite graph convolution module for inter-subnetwork translation (IST) governed by an adaptive weighted fusion (AWF) schedule. Because R2C's ablations toggle entire modules on or off, two fundamental design questions remain open: whether the learned branch must operate at subnetwork granularity with explicit convex-hull boundary extraction rather than globally at the node level, and whether adaptive fusion requires a steep temporal transition rather than a smooth or constant weighting.

\textbf{Controlled multi-axis ablations reveal that node-level hybrid fusion with fixed or smooth scheduling captures the vast majority of recovery performance without geometric partitioning.} We investigate these questions through Hybrid Adaptive Convolution (\textsc{HAC}), a streamlined architecture that fuses a parameter-free buffered virtual-force branch with a single global graph convolutional policy at the velocity level. Our systematic evaluation yields four principal findings:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item Fusing a global node-level learned branch with a parameter-free virtual-force heuristic under a constant $50/50$ weight (\textsc{HAC-Fixed}) retains $88.7\%$ to $90.7\%$ of R2C's reported coverage under $80\%$ node failure while achieving near-perfect recovery rates and $3\times$ to $5\times$ lower cross-seed variance than single-branch baselines.
  \item Directly applying a steep adaptive fusion schedule to this simplified architecture severely degrades recovery robustness under heavy node loss, reversing the trend observed in complex multipartite architectures.
  \item Recovery degradation under adaptive gating is governed by transition steepness rather than transition timing, as widening the sigmoid transition band restores near-optimal success rates by providing an implicit residual-correction training curriculum.
  \item Schedule smoothing rescues node-level hybrid control (\textsc{HAC}) but fails to rescue subnetwork-level rigid-translation hybrid control (\textsc{HSC}), cleanly separating a training-schedule bottleneck from an architectural expressiveness bottleneck.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Summary of Contributions}
The primary contributions of this paper are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item We formulate a three-axis taxonomy of the SCC design space spanning decision paradigm, spatial reasoning granularity, and trade-off strategy, positioning thirteen representative methods and isolating previously unexplored hybrid configurations.
  \item We propose \textsc{HAC}, a lightweight hybrid recovery architecture aided by a cardinality-invariant soft-minimum connectivity surrogate loss, and validate its resilience and variance-suppression properties across random and uniform deployments over five independent random seeds.
  \item We establish a theoretical and empirical account of hybrid fusion scheduling based on link-retention barriers and training-time autonomy exposure, deriving a falsifiable prediction that relates transition slope to closed-loop learning stability.
  \item We provide a complete comparative analysis separating node-level versus subnetwork-level hybrid expressiveness, accompanied by open-source simulation code, training scripts, and multi-seed evaluation logs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Non-Learning Self-Healing Methods}
\textbf{Critical-node compensation algorithms restore local links through cascaded neighbor movements but incur prohibitive coordination overhead as swarm scale grows.} Early self-healing protocols detect cut-vertices whose removal partitions the network and trigger sequential backup replacements \citep{abbasi2008movement,akkaya2009distributed,mi2015obstacle,zhang2024uavenabled}. While effective for isolated single-node faults, identifying critical nodes and coordinating multi-hop cascades scale poorly when simultaneous failures shatter the topology into numerous disjoint clusters.

\textbf{Rendezvous-point aggregation methods eliminate cut-vertex search but rely on static geometric rules that falter under unpredictable failure patterns.} Alternative heuristic schemes direct surviving UAVs or fractured subnetworks toward predetermined landmarks using Coulomb-inspired forces \citep{joshi2012autonomous}, round-table negotiation \citep{shriwastav2018roundtable}, or artificial potential fields \citep{chen2020sidr,wang2024apfcpp}. Although parameter-free and computationally lightweight, these hand-crafted rules lack global topological awareness and frequently stall in local equilibria or cause excessive coverage contraction under high failure ratios \citep{tosun2024dpkcr}.

\subsection{Learning-Based Self-Healing Methods}
\textbf{Graph neural network policies accelerate global reconnection but exhibit severe spatial collapse around high-degree nodes.} Posing connectivity recovery as a differentiable graph optimization problem enables end-to-end learning of coordinated trajectories \citep{karalias2020erdos,peng2025prioritized}. \citet{mou2022resilient} employ a relaxed GCN over virtual communication links, while \citet{guo2024intelligent} incorporate spatiotemporal multi-head attention. Despite fast convergence, standard message-passing schemes pull peripheral agents toward dense clusters, degrading coverage uniformity.

\textbf{Diffusion and differential-subgraph architectures improve spatial dispersion yet remain vulnerable to extreme topological fragmentation.} Recent works counter spatial collapse by introducing multi-hop diffusion convolutions \citep{lin2024multihop} or bipartite message passing over differential subgraphs \citep{lin2025multihop}. While these mechanisms encourage broader spatial spread, purely learned policies lack hard repulsive buffers to guarantee inter-agent spacing and exhibit high sensitivity to weight initialization when up to $80\%$ of the swarm is lost.

\subsection{Hybrid Heuristic--Learning Architectures and Positioning}
\label{sec:positioning}
\textbf{Hybrid recovery frameworks combine physical priors with learned graph representations to balance local spacing and global bridging.} \citet{peng2026r2c} proposed R2C, which pairs a buffered Hookean force field for intra-subnetwork expansion (ISE) with a multipartite spectral GCN for inter-subnetwork translation (IST) using Quickhull boundary extraction \citep{barber1996quickhull} and asymmetric spectral filtering \citep{liu2025asymmetric}, blended via a sigmoid adaptive weighted fusion (AWF) schedule \citep{elfwing2018sigmoid}. Elsewhere, \citet{li2025fast} combine distributed multi-agent reinforcement learning with artificial potential fields at the node level under fixed weighting, while \citet{wang2025uav} and related multi-robot coordination studies \citep{ye2022multiuav,mutzari2025heterogeneous,shi2025symmetry,kipf2016semi,zeng2023substructure} explore graph-based control without adaptive heuristic-learned velocity fusion.

\textbf{Our work isolates spatial reasoning granularity and schedule smoothness as independent design dimensions within the hybrid recovery paradigm.} Whereas prior studies evaluate hybrid modules as monolithic packages, we decompose the SCC design space along decision paradigm, spatial granularity, and trade-off scheduling. By holding the training objective and graph backbone fixed while systematically varying spatial granularity (node-level global vs.\ subnetwork-level rigid translation) and fusion slope, we identify the minimal architectural and scheduling conditions required for resilient recovery.

\section{Preliminaries and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{UAV Swarm Network Model}
\textbf{The surviving UAV swarm is modeled as a time-varying proximity graph over a bounded planar mission area.} Following \citet{peng2026r2c}, at discrete time step $t$, the swarm forms a temporal graph $G_t = (V_t, E_t, P_t)$, where $V_t = \{v_{1,t}, \dots, v_{|V_t|,t}\}$ denotes the surviving UAVs, $E_t$ denotes the active communication links, and $P_t = \{\mathbf{p}_{1,t}, \dots, \mathbf{p}_{|V_t|,t}\}$ with $\mathbf{p}_{i,t} \in \mathcal{A} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ represents their planar coordinates. Let $\mathbf{A}_t$ be the binary adjacency matrix and $\mathbf{L}_t = \mathbf{D}_t - \mathbf{A}_t$ be the graph Laplacian. The number of connected components $C_t$ equals the algebraic multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue of $\mathbf{L}_t$, and global connectivity holds if and only if $C_t = 1$.

\textbf{Communication links and ground coverage follow Euclidean disk models governed by air-to-air path loss.} An undirected link $(v_i, v_j) \in E_t$ exists if and only if the inter-UAV distance $d_{ij,t} = \|\mathbf{p}_{i,t} - \mathbf{p}_{j,t}\|_2$ satisfies $d_{ij,t} \le R_c$, where $R_c$ is the communication range derived from free-space air-to-air channel models \citep{ma2019wideband,ni2024path,wang2025flight}. Each UAV covers a circular disk $\mathcal{C}_i(t)$ of radius $R_{\mathrm{cov}}$, yielding a collective swarm coverage ratio $C_{\mathrm{cov}}(t) = \bigl|\bigcup_{v_i \in V_t} \mathcal{C}_i(t)\bigr| / |\mathcal{A}|$.

\subsection{Optimization Objective and Kinematic Constraints}
\textbf{The self-healing connectivity recovery objective jointly penalizes reconnection delay and post-recovery coverage loss.} Let $t_1$ denote the failure onset step and $t_r = \min\{t \ge t_1 : C_t = 1\}$ denote the earliest step at which full connectivity is restored within horizon $T$, giving a normalized recovery time ratio $T_{\mathrm{rec}} = (t_r - t_1)/T$. We seek a velocity control policy $\hat{V}_t = \{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{1,t}, \dots, \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{|V_t|,t}\}$ that minimizes the composite cost
\begin{equation}
  J_c = T_{\mathrm{rec}} + \bigl(1 - C_{\mathrm{cov}}(t_r)\bigr),
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
subject to four operational constraints: (C1) terminal connectivity $C_{t_r} = 1$; (C2) speed and arena bounds $\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{i,t}\|_2 \le \hat{v}_{\max}$ and $\mathbf{p}_{i,t} \in \mathcal{A}$; (C3) discrete-time Euler kinematics $\mathbf{p}_{i,t} = \mathbf{p}_{i,t_1} + \sum_{t_1 \le k < t} \Delta t \cdot \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{i,k}$; and (C4) link feasibility $d_{ij,t} \le R_c$ for all active intra-swarm transmissions. Minimizing $T_{\mathrm{rec}}$ requires inward convergence, whereas minimizing $1 - C_{\mathrm{cov}}(t_r)$ requires spatial dispersion.

\subsection{A Three-Axis Taxonomy of the Design Space}
\label{sec:taxonomy}
\textbf{Every self-healing recovery method can be categorized along three orthogonal design axes: decision paradigm, spatial granularity, and trade-off strategy.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Axis 1 (Decision Paradigm):} Motion commands are generated by a \emph{heuristic} rule with zero learnable parameters, a \emph{learned} parametric network trained via data or differentiable simulation, or a \emph{hybrid} controller that explicitly fuses heuristic and learned outputs.
  \item \textbf{Axis 2 (Spatial Reasoning Granularity):} Control reasoning operates at the \emph{node-level} using local neighborhood states, the \emph{subnetwork-level} where each connected component acts as a rigid or coordinated unit, or the \emph{global-level} where all surviving agents exchange context across the entire arena.
  \item \textbf{Axis 3 (Connectivity--Coverage Trade-Off Strategy):} Competing objectives are balanced via a \emph{fixed} constant weight, a \emph{staged} sequential switch, or a continuous \emph{adaptive} schedule parameterized by time or topological state.
\end{itemize}

\section{Motivating Experiments: Failure Modes of Single-Branch Designs}
\label{sec:motivating}

To motivate our hybrid architecture, we first isolate two purely learned baselines that share identical loss functions and network backbones but operate at opposite spatial granularities: node-level global convolution (\textsc{GAC}) and subnetwork-level rigid translation (\textsc{SAC}).

\subsection{Global-Level Learned Policy (GAC)}
\label{sec:gac}
\textbf{A purely learned global graph policy (\textsc{GAC}) achieves moderate reconnection capability but suffers from severe spatial collapse.} \textsc{GAC} constructs a $k$-nearest-neighbor ($k$-NN) graph over the Euclidean coordinates of all surviving UAVs regardless of communication range $R_c$, allowing message passing across disconnected components. A $4$-layer GCN with hidden dimension $128$ and a two-layer MLP head directly outputs per-node 2D velocities scaled via $\tanh$ by $\hat{v}_{\max}$ (without the unit-norm velocity projection used in hybrid fusion), trained end-to-end with equal weights on connectivity and coverage surrogate losses. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:gac-sac-random}, \textsc{GAC}'s recovery success rate drops from $49.0 \pm 0.6$ out of $50$ trials at $20\%$ failure to $19.8 \pm 5.5$ at $80\%$ failure, accompanied by an elevated average node degree ($\mathrm{AND} = 13.68$ at $20\%$ failure) that reflects severe spatial clustering around high-degree nodes.

\subsection{Subnetwork-Level Rigid Translation (SAC)}
\label{sec:sac}
\textbf{Restricting the learned policy to subnetwork-level rigid translation (\textsc{SAC}) prevents spatial clustering but causes deterministic recovery collapse under high failure rates.} \textsc{SAC} partitions surviving UAVs into connected components under $R_c$, pools each component into its centroid position and relative size, and applies the same $4$-layer GCN over a $k$-NN graph of component centroids to predict a single rigid 2D translation vector shared by all members of each subnetwork. Table~\ref{tab:gac-sac-random} demonstrates that \textsc{SAC} maintains a low, uniform node degree ($\mathrm{AND} = 6.82$ at $20\%$ failure), eliminating intra-cluster collapse. However, its recovery success rate plummets to $5.0 \pm 0.0$ out of $50$ at $60\%$ failure and $0.0 \pm 0.0$ at $80\%$ failure across all five seeds. Rigid component translation lacks the internal degrees of freedom needed to simultaneously bridge multiple scattered singletons without tearing existing links.

\subsection{Complementary Failure Modes Motivate Node-Level Hybridization}
\label{sec:takeaway}
\textbf{The opposite failure modes of \textsc{GAC} and \textsc{SAC} demonstrate that resilient recovery requires both node-level movement flexibility and an explicit local spacing prior.} \textsc{GAC} retains full $2|V_t|$ spatial degrees of freedom to bridge fragmented clusters but lacks a short-range repulsive mechanism to prevent over-concentration. Conversely, \textsc{SAC} preserves internal spacing by freezing relative intra-component positions but loses the ability to deform components toward multiple targets. This complementarity directly motivates fusing a parameter-free local virtual-force field with a node-level global GCN policy.

\section{Method: Hybrid Adaptive Convolution (HAC)}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Architecture Overview}
\textbf{Hybrid Adaptive Convolution (\textsc{HAC}) fuses a parameter-free buffered virtual-force heuristic with a single global graph convolutional network at the individual node velocity level.} Unlike multipartite subnetwork architectures that require Quickhull boundary extraction and cross-subgraph virtual edge construction, \textsc{HAC} operates directly on the homogeneous set of surviving UAVs. This streamlined design isolates the core benefit of heuristic--learned velocity coupling from complex geometric preprocessing.

\subsection{Heuristic Branch: Buffered Three-Zone Virtual-Force Field}
\label{sec:heuristic-branch}
\textbf{The heuristic branch regulates local inter-UAV spacing and arena containment through a parameter-free three-zone potential field.} Adopting the buffered virtual-spring formulation \citep{rychlewski1984hooke,peng2026r2c}, we partition the communication disk $(0, R_c]$ around an equilibrium buffer zone $[d_l, d_u] = [0.8 R_c, 0.9 R_c]$. The pairwise virtual force exerted on UAV $v_i$ by a communicating neighbor $v_j \in \mathcal{N}_i = \{v_j \in V_t : 0 < d_{ij,t} \le R_c\}$ is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}'_{ij,t} =
  \begin{cases}
    -\exp\!\left(-\alpha \cdot \dfrac{d_{ij,t}}{d_l}\right) \mathbf{u}_{ij,t}, & 0 \le d_{ij,t} < d_l \\[6pt]
    \mathbf{0}, & d_l \le d_{ij,t} \le d_u \\[4pt]
    \exp\!\left(-\beta \cdot \dfrac{R_c - d_{ij,t}}{R_c - d_u}\right) \mathbf{u}_{ij,t}, & d_u < d_{ij,t} \le R_c,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:heuristic-force}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{u}_{ij,t} = (\mathbf{p}_{j,t} - \mathbf{p}_{i,t}) / d_{ij,t}$ is the unit direction vector from $v_i$ to $v_j$, and $\alpha = \beta = 0.01$ govern the exponential decay rates in the repulsive zone $[0, d_l)$ and attractive buffer zone $(d_u, R_c]$. To prevent agents from exiting the mission area $\mathcal{A}$, any UAV within distance $d_{i,k} < d_e = 0.15 R_c$ of boundary edge $k \in \mathcal{B} = \{\text{left, right, bottom, top}\}$ experiences an inward normal repulsion $\mathbf{F}''_{i,t} = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{B}} \exp(-\delta \cdot d_{i,k})\,\mathbf{n}_k$ with $\delta = 0.01$. Summing $\mathbf{F}_{i,t} = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} \mathbf{F}'_{ij,t} + \mathbf{F}''_{i,t}$ and normalizing yields the heuristic velocity command $\mathbf{v}'_{i,t} = \hat{v}_{\max} \cdot \mathbf{F}_{i,t} / (\|\mathbf{F}_{i,t}\|_2 + \epsilon)$. Because Eq.~\eqref{eq:heuristic-force} contains no trainable weights, $\mathbf{v}'_{i,t}$ provides an invariant physical anchor throughout both training and inference.

\subsection{Learned Branch and Velocity-Level Adaptive Fusion}
\label{sec:learned-branch}
\label{sec:fusion}
\textbf{The learned branch propagates topological context across disconnected components via a global $k$-NN graph convolutional network.} At each step $t$, we construct a symmetric $k$-NN graph ($k=8$) over the Euclidean coordinates of all surviving UAVs $V_t$. Each node $v_i$ receives a $3$-dimensional input feature vector comprising its normalized planar coordinates and its normalized $k$-NN degree. This feature representation passes through an $L=4$ layer GCN with hidden dimension $128$ and ReLU activations, followed by a two-layer MLP head with $\tanh$ activation scaled by $\hat{v}_{\max}$ to produce the learned velocity vector $\mathbf{v}''_{i,t}$.

\textbf{Velocity-level fusion combines heuristic and learned commands through a tunable sigmoid schedule followed by unit-speed renormalization.} To govern the temporal handover from heuristic expansion to learned global translation, we define the time-dependent gating weights
\begin{equation}
  w_T(t) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\bigl(-k \cdot (t/T - s_0)\bigr)}, \qquad w_E(t) = 1 - w_T(t),
  \label{eq:awf-schedule}
\end{equation}
where $k > 0$ controls transition steepness and $s_0 \in (0, 1)$ specifies the crossover midpoint relative to horizon $T$. The executed velocity command $\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{i,t}$ is obtained by renormalizing the weighted sum:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{i,t} = \hat{v}_{\max} \cdot
  \frac{w_E(t)\,\mathbf{v}'_{i,t} + w_T(t)\,\mathbf{v}''_{i,t}}
       {\left\| w_E(t)\,\mathbf{v}'_{i,t} + w_T(t)\,\mathbf{v}''_{i,t} \right\|_2 + \epsilon}.
  \label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
We evaluate three primary configurations of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}: \textbf{\textsc{HAC-Fixed}}, which sets constant equal weights $w_E(t) = w_T(t) = 0.5$; \textbf{\textsc{HAC-Adaptive}}, which varies $(k, s_0)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:awf-schedule}; and the \textbf{\textsc{Learned-only}} ablation, which sets $w_T(t) = 1$ and $w_E(t) = 0$ inside Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion} so that $\mathbf{v}''_{i,t}$ undergoes exact unit-norm projection $\hat{v}_{\max} \mathbf{v}''_{i,t} / (\|\mathbf{v}''_{i,t}\|_2 + \epsilon)$ (distinguishing it from \textsc{GAC}, which uses unprojected $\tanh$ scaling).

\subsection{Cardinality-Invariant Training Objective and Algorithms}
\label{sec:objective}
\textbf{A cardinality-invariant soft-minimum connectivity surrogate eliminates component-size bias during differentiable multi-step rollout training.} Because the discrete component count $C_t$ is non-differentiable, we supervise inter-component bridging using a smooth soft-minimum distance surrogate over every pair of currently disconnected components $S_i, S_j$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{conn}}^{(i,j)} =
  -\tau \left( \log \sum_{\substack{u \in S_i \\ v \in S_j}}
  \exp\!\left(-\frac{d_{uv}}{\tau}\right) - \log(n_i n_j) \right),
  \label{eq:conn-loss}
\end{equation}
averaged over all disconnected component pairs, where $n_i = |S_i|$, $n_j = |S_j|$, and $\tau > 0$ is a temperature parameter. Standard log-sum-exp soft-minimum estimators omit the $-\log(n_i n_j)$ correction, introducing an additive distortion of $-\tau \log(n_i n_j)$ that systematically under-penalizes large components and yields spuriously negative loss values (verified numerically in Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). Including $-\log(n_i n_j)$ ensures exact cardinality invariance so that isolated singletons receive gradient magnitudes consistent with large subnetworks.

\textbf{Spatial coverage and movement efficiency are supervised jointly through differentiable grid-coverage and displacement regularizers.} We approximate area coverage over a uniform grid $G \subset \mathcal{A}$ via $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cov}} = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{\mathbf{g} \in G} \bigl(1 - \sigma\bigl((R_{\mathrm{cov}} - \min_i \|\mathbf{g} - \mathbf{p}_i\|_2)/\tau'\bigr)\bigr)$ and penalize excessive control effort via $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{move}} = \frac{1}{|V_t|}\sum_i \|\mathbf{p}_{i,t+1} - \mathbf{p}_{i,t}\|_2$. Throughout training, connectivity and coverage losses receive equal static weights of $0.5$, ensuring that all temporal trade-offs occur strictly at the velocity fusion layer in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}. Algorithms~\ref{alg:hac-train} and~\ref{alg:hac-deploy} summarize end-to-end differentiable rollout training and closed-loop deployment.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{HAC Differentiable Rollout Training (One Epoch)}
\label{alg:hac-train}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Learned policy $\pi_\theta$, training rollout length $T_{\mathrm{train}}$, weight schedule $w_T(\cdot)$
\State Sample failure ratio $r \sim \{0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8\}$, initialize positions $\mathbf{P}_0$, and remove failed nodes
\State $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} \gets 0$
\For{$t = 0, \dots, T_{\mathrm{train}}-1$}
  \State Identify connected components $\{S_m\}$ of $\mathbf{P}_t$ under communication range $R_c$
  \State Compute heuristic velocities $\mathbf{v}'$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:heuristic-force} \Comment{detached, zero trainable parameters}
  \State Construct global $k$-NN graph over $\mathbf{P}_t$ and evaluate $\mathbf{v}'' \gets \pi_\theta(\mathbf{P}_t)$ \Comment{learned branch}
  \State Evaluate gating weights $w_T \gets w_T(t)$ and $w_E \gets 1 - w_T$
  \State Fuse velocities $\hat{\mathbf{v}} \gets \text{Fuse}(\mathbf{v}', \mathbf{v}'', w_E, w_T)$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}
  \State Update positions $\mathbf{P}_{t+1} \gets \text{clip}(\mathbf{P}_t + \Delta t \cdot \hat{\mathbf{v}}, \; 0, \; L)$
  \State $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} \gets \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} + 0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{conn}}(\mathbf{P}_{t+1}) + 0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cov}}(\mathbf{P}_{t+1}) + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{move}}$
\EndFor
\State Backpropagate $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} / T_{\mathrm{train}}$ through $\pi_\theta$ and update $\theta$ via Adam with gradient clipping
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{HAC Inference Deployment Rollout}
\label{alg:hac-deploy}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Trained policy $\pi_\theta$, evaluation horizon $T$, weight schedule $w_T(\cdot)$
\For{$t = 0, \dots, T-1$}
  \If{$C_t = 1$ (swarm is fully connected)} \textbf{return} success, recovery step $t$
  \EndIf
  \State Compute heuristic velocities $\mathbf{v}'$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:heuristic-force}
  \State Evaluate learned velocities $\mathbf{v}'' \gets \pi_\theta(\mathbf{P}_t)$ \Comment{no gradient computation}
  \State Fuse velocities $\hat{\mathbf{v}} \gets \text{Fuse}(\mathbf{v}', \mathbf{v}'', 1-w_T(t), w_T(t))$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}
  \State Update positions $\mathbf{P}_{t+1} \gets \text{clip}(\mathbf{P}_t + \Delta t \cdot \hat{\mathbf{v}}, \; 0, \; L)$
\EndFor
\State \textbf{return} failure (horizon $T$ exhausted)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

Our ablation studies in Section~\ref{sec:ablation} demonstrate that applying a steep adaptive schedule ($k=30, s_0=0.125$) to \textsc{HAC} degrades recovery success under $80\%$ failure, whereas reducing the transition slope $k$ restores robustness. Here we establish two complementary mechanisms explaining why transition steepness governs hybrid recovery: an inference-time link-retention barrier in the heuristic branch and a training-time implicit curriculum governed by autonomy exposure.

\subsection{Link-Retention Barrier of the Heuristic Branch}
\label{sec:monotonicity}

\begin{proposition}[Link-retention barrier in the attractive buffer zone]
\label{prop:monotonicity}
Let $v_i, v_j$ be two connected UAVs occupying the attractive buffer zone $d_u < d_{ij,t} \le R_c$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:heuristic-force}, and suppose no external repulsive or boundary force acts on $v_i$ or $v_j$. Then, for any step size $0 < \Delta t < d_{ij,t} / (2\hat{v}_{\max})$, executing the heuristic branch alone ($w_E = 1, w_T = 0$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}) strictly decreases their inter-UAV distance according to $d_{ij,t+\Delta t} = d_{ij,t} - 2\Delta t\,\hat{v}_{\max} < R_c$, thereby guaranteeing that the existing communication link $(v_i, v_j) \in E_t$ cannot break at step $t + \Delta t$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:heuristic-force}, the pairwise force on $v_i$ from $v_j$ within $d_u < d_{ij,t} \le R_c$ is $\mathbf{F}'_{ij,t} = \exp\!\bigl(-\beta \frac{R_c - d_{ij,t}}{R_c-d_u}\bigr) \mathbf{u}_{ij,t}$ pointing from $v_i$ toward $v_j$. By symmetry, $\mathbf{F}'_{ji,t} = -\mathbf{F}'_{ij,t}$ points from $v_j$ toward $v_i$ with equal magnitude. Under the isolation assumption, $\mathbf{F}_{i,t} = \mathbf{F}'_{ij,t}$ and $\mathbf{F}_{j,t} = -\mathbf{F}'_{ij,t}$, which after unit-speed normalization yields $\mathbf{v}'_{i,t} = \hat{v}_{\max}\,\mathbf{u}_{ij,t}$ and $\mathbf{v}'_{j,t} = -\hat{v}_{\max}\,\mathbf{u}_{ij,t}$. Applying the one-step Euler update gives $d_{ij,t+\Delta t} = \|\mathbf{p}_{i,t} + \Delta t\,\mathbf{v}'_{i,t} - \mathbf{p}_{j,t} - \Delta t\,\mathbf{v}'_{j,t}\|_2 = \|(\mathbf{p}_{i,t} - \mathbf{p}_{j,t}) + 2\Delta t\, \hat{v}_{\max}\, \mathbf{u}_{ij,t}\|_2$. Substituting $\mathbf{p}_{i,t}-\mathbf{p}_{j,t} = -d_{ij,t}\,\mathbf{u}_{ij,t}$ yields $d_{ij,t+\Delta t} = |d_{ij,t} - 2\Delta t\, \hat{v}_{\max}| = d_{ij,t} - 2\Delta t\,\hat{v}_{\max} < d_{ij,t} \le R_c$ for all $0 < \Delta t < d_{ij,t}/(2\hat{v}_{\max})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Complementary roles of intra-subnetwork zones and handover risk]
Proposition~\ref{prop:monotonicity} establishes that the attractive buffer zone $(d_u, R_c]$ acts as a local restorative barrier that prevents existing communication links from fracturing, while the repulsive zone $[0, d_l)$ drives intra-subnetwork expansion to push boundary nodes outward toward disconnected components. Because the learned branch $\mathbf{v}''_{i,t}$ carries no structural barrier guarantee, prematurely suppressing $w_E(t)$ below a critical margin allows imperfect learned velocities to violate link-retention invariance and tear newly formed bridges.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Fusion Weight as an Implicit Training Curriculum}
\label{sec:curriculum}
\textbf{During end-to-end differentiable rollout training, the fusion schedule acts as an implicit curriculum that determines whether the learned branch masters residual correction before attempting autonomous control.} Because $\pi_\theta$ is trained directly through the fused velocity Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion} in Algorithm~\ref{alg:hac-train}, the temporal profile of $w_T(t)$ shapes the supervision landscape at every rollout step.

\begin{definition}[Autonomy exposure]
For a training rollout of length $T_{\mathrm{train}}$ and dominance threshold $\tau \in (0,1)$, the \emph{autonomy exposure} of schedule $w_T(\cdot; k, s_0)$ is the fraction of training steps during which the learned branch holds majority control:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{AE}(k, s_0; \tau) = \frac{1}{T_{\mathrm{train}}} \Bigl|
  \bigl\{\, t \in \{0,\dots,T_{\mathrm{train}}-1\} : w_T(t; k, s_0) > \tau \,\bigr\}
  \Bigr|.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

For $\tau = 0.5$, inverting Eq.~\eqref{eq:awf-schedule} between $w_T = 0.1$ and $w_T = 0.9$ yields a transition window of width $\Delta t \approx \frac{\ln(81)}{k}\,T_{\mathrm{train}} \approx \frac{4.394}{k}\,T_{\mathrm{train}}$. Consequently, a steep slope ($k=30$) compresses the handover into a narrow band and forces the randomly initialized GCN to operate nearly autonomously ($w_T \approx 1$) across most of the rollout, compounding open-loop trajectory errors. Conversely, a gentle slope ($k=5$, spanning $\approx 88\%$ of the $40$-step training rollout) maintains substantial heuristic guidance ($w_E > 0$) throughout training, enabling the GCN to learn stable residual corrections on top of a well-behaved physical baseline.

\begin{remark}[Boundary condition of curriculum smoothing]
The implicit-curriculum mechanism predicts two empirical phenomena verified in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}: first, smoothing the transition slope ($k=5, s_0=0.7$) restores \textsc{HAC}'s recovery rate under $80\%$ failure to match \textsc{HAC-Fixed}; second, schedule smoothing cannot rescue the subnetwork-level hybrid (\textsc{HSC}), because no training curriculum can overcome the representational inability of rigid component translation to express individual node deformations. We note that single-seed intermediate points ($k=10, s_0=0.125$ vs.\ $k=30, s_0=0.125$) exhibit minor non-monotonic fluctuations within the $\pm 5$ to $6$ trial variance typical of single-seed sweeps.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A Falsifiable Prediction for Multi-Seed Schedule Design}
\label{sec:prediction}
\textbf{The autonomy-exposure formulation yields a concrete, falsifiable prediction: schedules with distinct $(k, s_0)$ pairs but matched autonomy exposure $\mathrm{AE}(k, s_0; 0.5)$ will achieve statistically equivalent recovery robustness.} Testing this invariance across multi-seed contours of constant $\mathrm{AE}$ provides a direct diagnostic for future curriculum-scheduled hybrid controllers.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:expsetup}

\subsection{Simulation Environment and Failure Protocol}
\textbf{All architectures are evaluated across random and uniform swarm deployments under four node failure ratios up to $80\%$.} Mirroring the benchmark protocol of \citet{peng2026r2c}, we simulate a $1000 \times 1000\,\mathrm{m}^2$ mission area under two initial layouts: \textbf{random deployment} with $200$ UAVs sampled uniformly at random, and \textbf{uniform deployment} with $196$ UAVs initialized on a regular $14 \times 14$ grid. Each UAV operates with communication and coverage radii $R_c = R_{\mathrm{cov}} = 120\,\mathrm{m}$, maximum speed $\hat{v}_{\max} = 1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, time step $\Delta t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$, and an evaluation horizon of $T = 400$ steps. We evaluate four failure ratios $r \in \{20\%, 40\%, 60\%, 80\%\}$ via uniform random node removal. For every main configuration, we train $5$ independent random seeds and evaluate each seed across $50$ independent failure trials, reporting the cross-seed mean and standard deviation.

\subsection{Baselines and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:baselines-r2c}
\textbf{We compare \textsc{HAC} against purely heuristic, purely learned, subnetwork-level rigid, and published multipartite baselines across four topological and coverage metrics.} Our controlled baselines comprise: (i) \textbf{\textsc{Heuristic-only}}, executing Eq.~\eqref{eq:heuristic-force} in isolation ($w_E=1, w_T=0$); (ii) \textbf{\textsc{GAC-Fixed}} and \textbf{\textsc{SAC-Fixed}} from Section~\ref{sec:motivating}; (iii) \textbf{\textsc{Learned-only}}, setting $w_T=1, w_E=0$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}; and (iv) \textbf{\textsc{HSC}}, substituting \textsc{SAC}'s subnetwork-level rigid-translation GCN into the hybrid fusion loop. We also include the published metrics of \textbf{R2C} \citep{peng2026r2c} as an external reference point. Performance is quantified via four metrics:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Success/50:} Number of evaluation trials out of $50$ that restore full connectivity ($C_t = 1$) within $T = 400$ steps. Following standard protocol, non-recovering trials are excluded from conditional post-recovery averages.
  \item \textbf{REC ($\downarrow$):} Normalized recovery time ratio $T_{\mathrm{rec}} = (t_r - t_1)/T$ averaged over successful trials.
  \item \textbf{COV ($\uparrow$):} Swarm coverage ratio $C_{\mathrm{cov}}(t_r)$ at the instant of reconnection, evaluated via $5\,\mathrm{m}$ grid sampling.
  \item \textbf{AND:} Average node degree at $t_r$, serving as an inverse proxy for spatial dispersion (elevated AND indicates spatial clustering).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implementation and Seeding Protocol}
\textbf{All learned models share an identical $4$-layer GCN backbone and strict full-pipeline random seeding.} Every graph convolutional policy uses $L=4$ layers, hidden width $128$, $k=8$ nearest neighbors, movement cost weight $\lambda = 0.01$, and Adam optimization with learning rate $10^{-3}$ and gradient clipping at $1.0$ over $300$ epochs of $40$-step differentiable rollouts. Both environment randomness and PyTorch weight initialization (\texttt{torch.manual\_seed} and \texttt{torch.cuda.manual\_seed\_all}) are strictly locked per seed prior to model instantiation.

\subsection{Comparison with Multipartite Subnetwork Architectures}
\label{sec:r2c-comparison}
\textbf{\textsc{HAC-Fixed} retains $88.7\%$ to $90.7\%$ of the coverage reported by R2C under $80\%$ node failure despite omitting convex-hull boundary extraction, virtual multipartite edges, and adaptive schedule tuning.} Table~\ref{tab:r2c-comparison} compares \textsc{HAC-Fixed} against the published figures of R2C \citep{peng2026r2c}. At $20\%$ and $40\%$ failure rates, both methods achieve virtually identical coverage ($\ge 0.988$), with \textsc{HAC-Fixed} achieving faster recovery under uniform deployment ($\mathrm{REC}$ of $0.003$ vs.\ $0.037$ at $20\%$ failure and $0.037$ vs.\ $0.051$ at $40\%$ failure). \section{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablation}

\subsection{Synergistic Branch Contribution}
\label{sec:branch-ablation}
\textbf{Velocity-level hybrid fusion exhibits strict super-additive synergy under severe fragmentation rather than merely selecting the better constituent branch.} Comparing \textsc{HAC-Fixed} against its isolated branches in Tables~\ref{tab:main-random} and~\ref{tab:main-uniform} rules out simple branch dominance. Under random deployment at $80\%$ failure, \textsc{Heuristic-only} succeeds in $0.0/50$ trials and \textsc{Learned-only} succeeds in $12.0/50$ trials, yet their $50/50$ velocity fusion achieves $48.8/50$ successes (exceeding the sum of both constituent branches by $36.8$ trials). The heuristic branch prevents early local link breakage and spatial collapse (Proposition~\ref{prop:monotonicity}), creating well-spaced local clusters that the global GCN branch then steers into contact across long-range voids.

\subsection{Adaptive Fusion Schedule Steepness Sweep}
\label{sec:adaptive-ablation}
\textbf{Steep adaptive gating schedules degrade recovery robustness under $80\%$ node failure, whereas smoothing the transition slope restores near-optimal performance.} As shown in Table~\ref{tab:schedule-sweep}, transplanting the steep schedule $k=30, s_0=0.125$ into \textsc{HAC-Adaptive} reduces $80\%$-failure recovery success to $20/50$ (versus $48.8/50$ for \textsc{HAC-Fixed}). Shifting the transition midpoint later to $s_0=0.5$ while keeping the steep slope $k=30$ further degrades success to $8/50$, proving that transition timing alone is not the remedy. Conversely, progressively smoothing the slope to $k=10, s_0=0.5$ ($34/50$) and $k=5, s_0=0.7$ ($48/50$) recovers performance to match \textsc{HAC-Fixed} while achieving slightly faster recovery ($\mathrm{REC}=0.652$), higher coverage ($\mathrm{COV}=0.619$, $+1.5\text{ pp}$), and lower clustering ($\mathrm{AND}=2.46$) as detailed in Table~\ref{tab:schedule-detail}. Cross-deployment testing on the uniform layout confirms this trend, where smoothing from $(30, 0.125)$ to $(5, 0.7)$ boosts $80\%$-failure recovery from $7/50$ to $37/50$.

\label{sec:hsc}
\textbf{Replacing \textsc{HAC}'s node-level global GCN with a subnetwork-level rigid-translation GCN (\textsc{HSC}) causes total recovery collapse at $80\%$ failure that schedule smoothing cannot rescue.} To test whether smooth scheduling can compensate for restricted spatial expressiveness, we evaluate \textsc{HSC}, which fuses the heuristic virtual-force branch with \textsc{SAC}'s subnetwork-level rigid translation policy via Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}. This counter-experiment cleanly separates scheduling instability from architectural expressiveness: when a policy's output space is restricted to rigid subnetwork translation, no curriculum schedule can supply the node-level degrees of freedom required under extreme fragmentation.

\subsection{Initialization Robustness and Variance Suppression}
\label{sec:seed-fix}
\textbf{Coupling the learned policy with a parameter-free virtual-force heuristic suppresses cross-seed initialization variance by a factor of three to five under extreme failure.} Purely learned graph policies exhibit high sensitivity to random weight initialization when evaluated on multi-step open-loop rollouts: across five properly locked random seeds at $80\%$ failure, \textsc{GAC-Fixed} and \textsc{Learned-only} display large success-rate standard deviations of $\pm 5.5$ and $\pm 3.5$ trials in random deployment (and $\pm 6.1$ and $\pm 6.5$ in uniform deployment). Moreover, an unseeded single run of \textsc{GAC-Fixed} at $20\%$ failure yielded an optimistic outlier ($\mathrm{COV}=0.830, \mathrm{AND}=11.87$) compared to the true five-seed distribution ($\mathrm{COV}=0.747\pm0.009, \mathrm{AND}=13.68\pm0.28$). In contrast, \textsc{HAC-Fixed} anchors every rollout step to the deterministic physical force field in Eq.~\eqref{eq:heuristic-force}, compressing the $80\%$-failure success standard deviation to $\pm 0.98$ (random) and $\pm 2.0$ (uniform) while holding coverage standard deviation to $\pm 0.005$.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Trajectory-Level Complementarity and Scheduling Guidelines}
\textbf{Simple velocity-level hybrids succeed because local virtual forces and global graph convolutions resolve non-overlapping failure modes at every step of an individual trajectory.} The virtual-force branch guarantees local link retention and collision avoidance within connected components (Proposition~\ref{prop:monotonicity}) but is blind to components beyond $R_c$. Conversely, the global $k$-NN GCN perceives the relative geometry of disconnected subnetworks across the entire arena but tends to over-cluster nodes if left unchecked. Fusing both branches at the velocity level allows the heuristic term to continuously regularize intra-component spacing while the GCN steers component centroids into reconnection range.

\textbf{Practitioners designing adaptive hybrid controllers should verify output-space expressiveness before tuning handover schedules, and default to constant or wide-transition schedules.} Comparing \textsc{HAC} and \textsc{HSC} yields a clear engineering principle: adaptive schedule tuning is only meaningful when the learned branch possesses node-level actuation authority. Furthermore, because steep temporal handovers degrade end-to-end training by inflating autonomy exposure, zero-tuning constant fusion ($50/50$) or wide-window smooth scheduling ($k=5$) provides a much safer default than sharp stage switches.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
\textbf{Closing the remaining $6.2$ to $7.7\text{ pp}$ coverage gap under $80\%$ failure and extending differentiable rollout horizons represent promising avenues for future research.} First, while \textsc{HAC-Fixed} retains roughly $90\%$ of R2C's reported coverage under $80\%$ node loss without boundary-hull machinery, introducing lightweight node-level boundary indicators or increasing GCN capacity from $4$ layers ($128$ hidden units) toward deeper spectral architectures could close the remaining $6.2$ to $7.7\text{ pp}$ margin without sacrificing node-level simplicity. Second, due to backpropagation-through-time memory constraints, our models were trained on $40$-step rollouts and evaluated over $400$-step horizons; curriculum-extended rollout horizons or truncated gradient windowing could further improve late-stage trajectory efficiency. Finally, conducting multi-seed sweeps across iso-contours of autonomy exposure $\mathrm{AE}(k, s_0; 0.5)$ will directly test the theoretical invariance predicted in Section~\ref{sec:prediction}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper systematically disentangled the roles of decision paradigm, spatial reasoning granularity, and temporal fusion scheduling in self-healing UAV swarm connectivity recovery. By introducing Hybrid Adaptive Convolution (\textsc{HAC}), we demonstrated that fusing a parameter-free buffered virtual-force heuristic with a single global node-level GCN under a constant $50/50$ weighting outperforms purely heuristic, purely learned, and subnetwork-level rigid baselines across random and uniform deployments, retaining $88.7\%$ to $90.7\%$ of the coverage reported by complex multipartite architectures under $80\%$ node failure without boundary-hull extraction. Furthermore, we showed theoretically and empirically that steep adaptive fusion schedules impair end-to-end learning by prematurely exposing the learned branch to high autonomy, whereas smooth transition schedules restore robustness for node-level policies while failing to rescue expressively constrained subnetwork-level rigid controllers. Together with our cardinality-invariant connectivity loss formulation and open-source benchmark suite, these findings provide a rigorous foundation for designing simple, resilient hybrid swarm controllers.

\section{Implementation Details and Methodological Verification Log}
\label{app:deviations}

To ensure full reproducibility and clarify experimental design choices relative to prior literature \citep{peng2026r2c}, we document all simulation specifications and numerical verifications below:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Failure-node sampling:} Failed UAVs at ratios $\{20\%, 40\%, 60\%, 80\%\}$ are sampled uniformly at random without replacement from the initial deployed swarm.
  \item \textbf{Cardinality-invariant connectivity loss verification:} As derived in Eq.~\eqref{eq:conn-loss}, omitting the $-\log(n_i n_j)$ normalization term in a log-sum-exp soft-minimum surrogate introduces a severe component-size bias. On a controlled synthetic benchmark featuring two disconnected components separated by an identical true minimum distance ($\approx 100$ to $108\,\mathrm{m}$) under two cardinality configurations ($3\times3$ nodes vs.\ $30\times30$ nodes), the unnormalized log-sum-exp estimator returned $2.85$ for the $3\times3$ pair and $-230.58$ for the $30\times30$ pair. In contrast, our cardinality-invariant estimator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:conn-loss} returned $112.71$ and $109.54$, respectively, accurately tracking the true geometric separation regardless of cluster size.
  \item \textbf{Boundary buffer specification:} The arena boundary repulsion buffer is set to $d_e = 0.15 R_c = 18\,\mathrm{m}$, matching the scale of the inter-UAV equilibrium band $d_u - d_l = 0.1 R_c = 12\,\mathrm{m}$.
  \item \textbf{Differentiable rollout horizon:} Training uses $T_{\mathrm{train}} = 40$ differentiable rollout steps per epoch for backpropagation-through-time tractability, while evaluation runs for the full horizon $T = 400$ steps.
  \item \textbf{Grid-based coverage evaluation:} Swarm coverage $C_{\mathrm{cov}}(t)$ is evaluated over a deterministic uniform grid at $5\,\mathrm{m}$ spatial resolution across the $1000 \times 1000\,\mathrm{m}^2$ arena.
  \item \textbf{Full-pipeline random seeding:} Every multi-seed experiment locks Python, NumPy, CPU PyTorch (\texttt{torch.manual\_seed}), and CUDA PyTorch (\texttt{torch.cuda.manual\_seed\_all}) generators prior to both network initialization and environment reset.
\end{itemize}
